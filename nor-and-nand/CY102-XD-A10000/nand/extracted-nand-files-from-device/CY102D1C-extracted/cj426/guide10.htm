Standard English

Unless otherwise stated, the words and senses recorded in this dictionary are all part of standard English; that is, they are in normal use in both speech and writing everywhere in the world, at many different levels of formality, ranging from official documents to casual conversation. Some words, however, are appropriate only in particular contexts, and these are labeled accordingly. The technical term for a particular level of use in language is register.

The New Oxford American Dictionary uses the following register labels:

&F973;formal&F974;: normally used only in writing, in contexts such as official documents.

&F973;informal&F974;: normally used only in contexts such as conversations or letters between friends.

&F973;dated&F974;: no longer used by the majority of English speakers, but still encountered occasionally, especially among the older generation.

&F973;archaic&F974;: very old-fashioned language, not in ordinary use at all today, but sometimes used to give a deliberately old-fashioned effect, or found in works of the past that are still widely read.

&F973;historical&F974;: still used today, but only to refer to some practice or artifact that is no longer part of the modern world.

&F973;literary&F974;: found only or mainly in literature written in an “elevated” style.

&F973;poetic&F974;: found only or mainly in poetry.

&F973;technical&F974;: normally used only in technical and specialist language, though not necessarily restricted to any specific subject field.

&F973;rare&F974;: not in normal use.

&F973;humorous&F974;: used with the intention of sounding funny or playful.

&F973;dialect&F974;: not used in standard American English, but still widely used in certain local regions of the United States.

&F973;offensive&F974;: language that is likely to cause offense, particularly racial offense, whether the speaker intends it or not.

&F973;derogatory&F974;: language intended to convey a low opinion or cause personal offense.

&F973;vulgar slang&F974;: informal language that may cause offense, often because it refers to the bodily functions of sexual activity or excretion, which are still widely regarded as taboo.
