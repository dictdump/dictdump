“One can ‘smoke’ them like a cigarette, and they last three
to four hours,” said Elisabeth Guertler, a hotelier and
leading Viennese socialite who oversaw the ball. The
pragmatic approach underscored tobacco-loving Austria's
reluctance to join the accelerating anti-smoking movement
that has been sweeping parts of Europe. But Ioan Holender,
director of the venerable 135-year-old State Opera House
which hosts the Opera Ball, defended the smoking ban. “We
are not a ballroom. We are a theater,” he told reporters.
“The entire Scala in Milan enforces a total prohibition of
smoking.” Guertler said she decided to make the nicotine gum
and inhalers available to avoid troubling smokers at the
ball, which annually draws about 4,500 well-heeled guests
who pay dearly for the privilege. 　(Read by Mari Yamada)
